## 1.0.0 (October 2026)

Haystack Needle reads public meeting agendas across Santa Clara County as they're posted and emails organizers when an item matches what they follow: a surveillance contract, a policing agreement, a rent ordinance. It's built to catch an item at its first public appearance, often a committee referral or a consent-calendar line, while there's still time to organize.

There's nothing to download. You sign up once, and alerts come by email.

### What it reads

- **County and regional:** the Santa Clara County Board of Supervisors with its committees, boards and commissions; VTA; Valley Water's board and committees; the Midpeninsula Regional Open Space District (Midpen); and the Open Space Authority.
- **Cities:** Campbell, Cupertino, Gilroy, Los Altos, Los Altos Hills, Milpitas, Morgan Hill, Mountain View, Palo Alto, San José, Santa Clara, Saratoga and Sunnyvale, with their councils and the committees and commissions they post.

It reads each item's title and text and, where a site posts them, the staff reports, contracts and resolutions attached to it, not just the agenda's cover. It checks every source every 2 hours, and hourly in the three days before a meeting, to catch amended agendas and late memos.

### What you get

- **Watchlists you pick:** surveillance, policing contracts, immigration enforcement, tenant protections, jails, data centers, mobile crisis response, and housing and homelessness. You can also pick single terms from them (Flock, say), add terms of your own, and choose which cities and agencies you hear about (all of them if you don't choose).
- **Fast alerts where it counts:** matches for surveillance, policing contracts and immigration enforcement are emailed right away. The rest come in one morning digest, usually by 7 a.m.
- **Items that move:** when a matter goes from committee to council in San José, Sunnyvale, Mountain View, Santa Clara, Cupertino or Valley Water, the alert says it moved forward instead of looking like a new item.
- **Useful and Noise links** in every alert, so the watchlists get better over time.
- **Your own emails:** one message to you alone, never a group email. Your first email says what you picked. To change it or stop, reply to any alert.

### Signing up

Fill in the sign-up form, which is end-to-end encrypted and asks for no name: just your email address and what you'd like to follow. Each sign-up is approved by hand, and you'll hear about matches found after you join.

### Privacy

- Your email address is kept only in an encrypted GitHub secret. It never appears in this public repository, its logs or its public state file, which knows each person only by a scrambled code.
- Nothing tracks whether you open an email or click a link. A Useful or Noise vote is counted without recording who sent it.
- Alerts are sent from Proton Mail.

### Not yet

- **Los Gatos:** its agenda emails say an agenda is posted but don't include it, so Los Gatos items aren't matched yet.
- **Monte Sereno and the County Board of Education:** both have been asked to email their agendas, and reading starts with the first one that arrives.
- **School district boards and community college districts** come in a later release. So many of them turn automated readers away that they'd take most of the work.
- **Not covered yet:** the Housing Authority, Central Fire and the El Camino Healthcare District.
- **Agendas without staff reports:** for Palo Alto (read from Civic Band, which collects its agendas), Saratoga, and Campbell's and Los Altos Hills' commissions and committees, only the agenda itself is matched.
- **Slow sites:** the county's agenda site asks for a minute between requests, so a full Board of Supervisors agenda takes most of a day to read (titles first, then attachments, soonest meetings first). The Campbell City Council's staff reports can take a few runs, since that site limits downloads.
- It doesn't read minutes, video or transcripts.
