Free agenda alerts for Santa Clara County organizers

Haystack Needle

Haystack Needle reads public meeting agendas across Santa Clara County as they're posted, and emails you when an item matches what you work on: a surveillance contract, a policing agreement, a rent ordinance.

It's built to catch an item the first time it shows up, often on a committee agenda or a consent calendar, while there's still time to organize. There's nothing to download: you sign up once, and alerts come by email.

Sign up

Opens an end-to-end encrypted form on CryptPad. It asks for your email address and what you want to follow, not your name. Each sign-up is approved by hand.

What it reads

The agendas of these councils, boards and agencies, with the committees and commissions they post. It reads each item's title and text and, where a site posts them, the staff reports, contracts and resolutions attached to it, not just the agenda's cover. It checks for new and amended agendas several times a day, and more often in the three days before a meeting.

What it watches for

Eight watchlists, each a set of words and phrases. Pick whole watchlists, single terms from them (just Flock, say), or both, and add words of your own.

Surveillance Right away

Flock, license plate readers (ALPR), ShotSpotter, real-time crime centers, drones as first responders, surveillance use policies

Policing contracts Right away

Law enforcement services, police services contracts, Sheriff's Office contracts

Immigration enforcement Right away

ICE, 287(g), immigration detainers, SB 54, sanctuary, rapid response networks

Tenant protections Morning digest

Rent stabilization, just cause eviction, tenant relocation, anti-displacement, right to counsel

Jails Morning digest

Main Jail, Elmwood, solitary confinement, juvenile hall, pretrial release, in-custody deaths

Data centers Morning digest

Data centers, hyperscale, colocation, large load, backup generators

Mobile crisis response Morning digest

Mobile crisis, co-responders, unarmed response, PERT, Trusted Response Urgent Support Team

Housing and homelessness Morning digest

Encampments, safe parking, interim housing, Homekey, Housing Element, RHNA

These are a few terms from each. See every term.

What you get

  • Only what you picked: the watchlists, terms and places you chose, or every place if you don't choose.
  • Right away when it can't wait: matches for surveillance, policing contracts and immigration enforcement are emailed as soon as they're found. The rest, including your own words, come in one morning digest, usually by 7 a.m.
  • What you need to act: each alert names the meeting and the item, quotes the words that matched and says where they were (the item's title, its text or a page of a staff report), links the agenda, and says how to send public comment when the agenda does.
  • Items that move: when a matter goes from committee to council in San José, Sunnyvale, Mountain View, Santa Clara, Cupertino or Valley Water, the alert says it moved forward instead of looking like a new item.
  • Email to you alone: one message to you, never a group email. Your first email says what you picked. To change it or stop, reply to any alert.
  • Useful and Noise links in every alert, so the watchlists get better over time.

How signing up works

  1. Fill in the form. It's end-to-end encrypted, on CryptPad. It asks for your email address, the watchlists and terms you want, any words of your own, and which places (all of them if you leave it blank). It doesn't ask your name.
  2. Drew Siegler, who runs Haystack Needle, approves each sign-up by hand.
  3. Your first email says what you picked. After that, you hear about matches found after you joined, not earlier ones. Alerts come from alerts@andrewsiegler.com, so add it to your contacts to keep them out of spam.
  4. To change what you get or stop, reply to any alert.

Privacy

  • Your email address is kept in the encrypted form, in an encrypted GitHub secret and on Drew's own computer. It never appears in Haystack Needle's public code, its logs or its public state file, which knows each person only by a scrambled code.
  • Nothing tracks whether you open an email or click a link. A Useful or Noise vote is an email you choose to send, and it's counted without recording who sent it.
  • Alerts are sent from Proton Mail.
  • This page has no analytics, cookies or outside scripts. GitHub, which hosts it, sees visitors' IP addresses, as any web host does.

Not covered yet

  • Los Gatos: its agenda emails say an agenda is posted but don't include it, so Los Gatos items aren't matched yet.
  • Monte Sereno and the County Board of Education have been asked to email their agendas. Reading starts with the first one that arrives.
  • School district and community college boards come in a later release.
  • The Housing Authority, Central Fire and the El Camino Healthcare District aren't read yet.
  • Agendas without staff reports: for Palo Alto, Saratoga, and Campbell's and Los Altos Hills' commissions and committees, only the agenda itself is matched.
  • Slow sites: the county's agenda site asks for a minute between requests, so a full Board of Supervisors agenda takes most of a day to read. The Campbell City Council's staff reports can take a few tries, since that site limits downloads.
  • It doesn't read minutes, video or transcripts.
